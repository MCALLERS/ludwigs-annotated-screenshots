MAC → IPAD → YOUR CONVERSATION

Start with two apps.

Capture on your Mac. Mark up on your iPad. Tap Done, then paste.

These steps apply to the current App Store release and Mac companion 0.5.1. You need an Apple silicon Mac with macOS 14 or later, an iPad with iPadOS 18 or later, and a trusted private network. Apple Pencil is optional.

1. Install on your Mac

Download the free Mac companion. Open the DMG, drag Ludwig’s Annotated Screenshots to Applications, and open it. Choose Set up screenshot folder. Allow Documents and Local Network access when prompted. Keep the Mac app open.

2. Install on your iPad

Get Ludwig’s on the App Store. It is free and available in the United States storefront. No TestFlight or Ludwig account is required. Open the iPad app and tap Connect your Mac → Scan Mac QR code.

3. Pair your devices

Connect both devices to the same trusted private network. On Mac, choose Connect iPad. Scan that QR from inside Ludwig’s on iPad and allow Camera and Local Network access. Keep the QR private: it contains your pairing key.

Using a different Mac or a different network? Open Connect iPad on the Mac you are using and scan its fresh QR. Pairing with a Mac does not pair your iPad with every Mac on your Apple Account.

4. Capture, mark, paste

  1. Press Shift–Command–4 on Mac and select an area. Do not hold Control: that sends the screenshot to the clipboard instead of the watched folder.
  2. Draw on the screenshot with Pencil or enable Finger drawing. Use two fingers to pan and zoom.
  3. For a typed or dictated note, choose Text / voice, tap the image, enter your words, and tap Add note. Dictation uses the microphone on Apple’s keyboard.
  4. Tap Done. Wait for Copied on your Mac, then press Command–V in a conversation or app that accepts images.

For a batch, use Done & Next, then attach the PNGs from Documents → Screenshot Tool → Ready on Mac. The clipboard holds the latest image. Clear queue resets the count without deleting your files.

If pairing fails

Keep the Mac awake and both apps open. Check Local Network permission on both devices. On iPad, look under Settings → Privacy & Security → Local Network; on Mac, under System Settings → Privacy & Security → Local Network.

Hotel, office guest, and café Wi-Fi can isolate devices even when the network name matches. The current release cannot get around that router restriction. Use a trusted private network or your own hotspot and scan a fresh QR. Nearby encrypted connectivity is in development; it is not yet in the downloads above.

Safari remains a drawing-only alternative: scan the Mac QR with the iPad Camera, open in Safari, and optionally use Share → Add to Home Screen. It has the same private-network requirement.

Need more help?

Report a problem with your Mac/iPad versions and the error message. Do not post private screenshots, pairing links, or QR codes. Read privacy and storage details.
