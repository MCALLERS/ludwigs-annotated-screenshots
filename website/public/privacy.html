YOUR SCREENSHOTS, EXPLAINED.

Privacy & storage.

Your Mac. Your iPad. A direct connection between your devices. You choose what to share next.

Effective 11 November 2026. Covers Mac 0.6.0, native iPad versions 1.0 and 1.1, and the Safari companion. Nearby mode requires iPad 1.1, available on the US App Store as verified 12 November 2026.

What the app does

The Mac companion reads screenshots from a local folder and transfers selected images to a paired iPad on the local network. The iPad returns a flattened, annotated PNG. The Mac saves it in Ready and writes it to the clipboard. We do not operate an upload server, analytics service, advertising service, or account system for this app.

Stored on your devices

  • Mac: originals and annotated images are in Documents/Screenshot Tool. Pairing keys, completion receipts, capture identifiers, session boundaries, and screenshot-settings backups are in Library/Application Support/Screenshot Tool.
  • Native iPad: pairing credentials are stored in Keychain. Draft drawings, text notes, cached screenshots, and pending exports are in the app’s local container, excluded from backup where configured by the app. Keychain entries can persist after app removal, depending on iPadOS behavior.
  • Safari: pairing cookies and local draft/queue state are stored in Safari website data.
  • Clipboard: the last completed image and its local file URL are copied on the Mac. System clipboard features and other apps may read or synchronize this content under your device settings.

Files are not automatically deleted from the Mac. Starting a new session resets the visible queue without deleting files. After a separate preview and confirmation, the native iPad app can ask the Mac companion to move previous-session PNGs to the Mac’s Trash. The current session is retained; files in Trash can be moved back through Finder. Local iPad drafts and cached copies are retained after Mac cleanup. You can also manage the files directly in Finder. Deleting the iPad app removes its local app data; clear Safari website data to remove browser drafts and pairing. Removing a file from a synced Documents folder also follows your sync provider’s behavior.

Network and permissions

Nearby mode (Mac 0.6.0 and iPad 1.1+) authenticates both devices using the pairing key and encrypts transfers with Apple’s TLS implementation. Bonjour advertises a derived identifier, not the pairing key; nearby devices may observe that identifier. No developer-operated relay receives your screenshots.

Safari / iPad 1.0 mode uses unencrypted local HTTP authenticated by a pairing key. The Mac keeps this legacy service available for compatibility. Use it only on a trusted private network: a party able to observe its traffic may see images or credentials. Nearby encryption does not protect simultaneous legacy HTTP traffic. Do not expose or port-forward the service to the Internet. A QR code or pairing link grants access to the local screenshot library; keep it private.

Camera access is used to scan the Mac’s pairing QR code. Local Network access connects to the Mac. The app does not record or upload camera footage. The Mac may request Documents access to read and save screenshots.

Optional keyboard Dictation

You can type a screenshot note or use Apple’s Dictation microphone on the iPad keyboard. Ludwig does not start a recorder, retain raw audio, or send audio to a transcription provider. The app receives the resulting text, saves placed notes with the local draft, and includes them in the finished PNG. Apple’s Dictation processing, availability, and privacy settings apply; not every language or device works offline. See Apple’s iPad Dictation guide.

Third parties

The app does not send screenshots to an AI provider. You choose when and where to paste or upload them. Those services’ policies apply after you share. iCloud Drive or another backup or sync service may independently copy the Documents folder if enabled. Apple may collect TestFlight diagnostics and tester feedback under its own policies. GitHub hosts downloads, source, and public issue reports under its policies. Cloudflare hosts the public information website; it does not receive screenshots from the app.

This website

This website is hosted by Cloudflare and loads its styles, illustrations, and animation code from this site. It does not include an analytics SDK, advertising tracker, or account system. If you choose to play or pause page motion, that preference is saved only in your browser. The footer’s “Use device motion setting” button clears that choice. Cloudflare processes ordinary network request information to deliver the website. Download links take you to GitHub; the native iPad app is distributed through Apple’s App Store. Optional beta builds use TestFlight. Their respective policies apply to those services.

Contact

For general privacy questions, open a GitHub issue without including private images or connection keys. For a security vulnerability, use the repository’s private vulnerability reporting feature.
